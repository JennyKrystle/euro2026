💡 Buy Swiss Half Fare Cards before your first Swiss train

CHF 150 each (2026 price), valid 1 month, sold online at sbb.ch. It halves nearly every train, bus, boat and mountain railway for the next 10 days: 50% off the full Jungfraujoch round trip, the Matterhorn gondola and Pilatus. Audited against the Swiss Travel Pass: the Half Fare Card wins by roughly CHF 250 per person on this itinerary.

Good To Know · Money & Practical

The small stuff that makes the trip smooth

💶 Currencies

NL, BE and DE use the euro. Switzerland uses Swiss francs. Cards and phone-tap work almost everywhere: ~EUR 150 + CHF 150 in cash between you is plenty. At ATMs always choose "charge in local currency".

🏨 Pay-at-property

Antwerp city tax EUR 42.96 pp · Interlaken resort fee ~CHF 31.50 · Zermatt city tax CHF 36. Small amounts, zero surprises at reception.

🧥 September weather

Lowlands 12–20°C with drizzle chances, bring the compact umbrella. Swiss valleys 8–18°C. Summits at or below freezing: fleece, windproof jacket, beanie, gloves. Comfortable shoes are the most important item in the suitcase.

📱 Connectivity

One Europe-region eSIM that includes Switzerland covers all 20 days. Apps to install now: NS (NL), SNCB (BE), DB Navigator (DE) and SBB, the one you will live in for ten days.

🚆 Trains, simply

Only three legs need advance booking: the Eurocity Direct to Antwerp (nsinternational.com) and the two int.bahn.de legs, Antwerp → Cologne and Cologne → Interlaken. Everything else, including all Swiss trains, is turn-up-and-go with the Half Fare Card.

🚨 Essentials

Emergency number is 112 everywhere. Pharmacies show a green cross. Tap water is drinkable in all four countries, Swiss street fountains too. Shops close Sundays in Germany and Switzerland, stations and airports excepted.
