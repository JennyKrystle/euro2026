Honest note
I placed the ones whose neighbourhoods I can vouch for. For the rest, tap the pin the day before to confirm the branch nearest you and that it is open: several of these are small independents with short or changing hours, and a few are chains with multiple locations.
Fries verdict
Fabel Friet and Manneken Pis are both excellent and both have queues. Doing both in three days is a lot of fries: pick Fabel Friet, it is 6 minutes from your bed.

🧥 What to wear up the mountain

Findeln sits around 2,050m, roughly 450m above Zermatt, so it runs 3 to 4°C colder than the village. Late September means about 8 to 12°C in the shade but 18 to 20°C on a sunny terrace, so layers you can peel off matter more than one warm coat.

Wear: a base layer, a fleece or light knit, and a packable windproof jacket. Running shoes are fine here in dry weather, the path is maintained gravel and dirt rather than a technical trail. If it has rained, take shorter steps and ride the funicular back up from Findeln instead of the long forest descent, which is where mud and wet roots appear. Sunglasses and high SPF, UV is fierce at altitude even when it feels cool. A beanie and thin gloves for the morning funicular.

No dress code to worry about. It is a mountain farmhouse, hiking clothes are completely normal, and everyone is there for the view rather than the outfit.

Good To Know · Money & Practical

The small stuff that makes the trip smooth

💶 Currencies

NL, BE and DE use the euro. Switzerland uses Swiss francs. Cards and phone-tap work almost everywhere: ~EUR 150 + CHF 150 in cash between you is plenty. At ATMs always choose "charge in local currency".

🏨 Pay-at-property

Antwerp city tax EUR 42.96 pp · Interlaken resort fee ~CHF 31.50 · Zermatt city tax CHF 36. Small amounts, zero surprises at reception.

🧥 September weather

Lowlands 12–20°C with drizzle chances, bring the compact umbrella. Swiss valleys 8–18°C. Summits at or below freezing: fleece, windproof jacket, beanie, gloves. Comfortable shoes are the most important item in the suitcase.

📥 How to keep this guide with you

iPhone: open in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It opens like an app and works with no signal. Android: open in Chrome, tap the three dots, then Download or Add to Home screen. Everyone should do this before flying. Every barcode, map pin and booking reference lives inside the file itself, so it all works at 10,000 metres or on a Swiss mountain with no bars. The map pins need signal to open Google Maps, but the guide never does.

📱 Connectivity · the Solis rules

One Solis, three phones. It shares to up to 10 devices and doubles as a power bank, so it covers the whole crew across all four countries. Three habits make it work: (1) agree each morning who carries it, because whoever holds it holds everyone's internet, so if you split up in Zermatt or at the beach the others are offline; (2) charge it every night alongside the phones, 16 hours of battery disappears faster when it is also charging someone's iPhone; (3) download offline Google Maps for Amsterdam, Antwerp, Brugge, Gent, Cologne, Interlaken, Zermatt, Lucerne and Zurich before you fly, plus save this guide offline, so a flat Solis is an inconvenience rather than an emergency. Apps to install now: NS (NL), SNCB (BE), DB Navigator (DE) and SBB, the one you will live in for ten days.

🚆 Trains, simply

Only three legs need advance booking: the Eurocity Direct to Antwerp (nsinternational.com) and the two int.bahn.de legs, Antwerp → Cologne and Cologne → Interlaken. Everything else, including all Swiss trains, is turn-up-and-go with the Half Fare Card.

🚨 Essentials

Emergency number is 112 everywhere. Pharmacies show a green cross. Tap water is drinkable in all four countries, Swiss street fountains too. Shops close Sundays in Germany and Switzerland, stations and airports excepted.
