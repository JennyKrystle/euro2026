07:15

🛬 Land at Schiphol 📍

Immigration first (your Schengen entry stamp), then bags. Take your time, today has no timed tickets.

08:45

🚆 Train Schiphol → Amsterdam Centraal 🧭 Route

15 minutes, every few minutes. Tap a contactless card at the gates (OVpay, ~EUR 4.90) and tap out at Centraal. Then tram 2 or 12 to Koningsplein📍 and 2 minutes on foot to the hotel. Drop bags at reception.

10:30

☕ Coffee + the Bloemenmarkt 📍

The floating flower market is literally at your doorstep. Ease in.

12:00

🍟 Lunch at Fabel Friet 📍 + the famous cookie

The TikTok fries, parmesan + truffle mayo, 6 minutes away on Runstraat. Then dessert two streets over: the warm Van Stapele📍 chocolate cookie, the most famous cookie in the Netherlands, 3 minutes from your hotel.

13:30

😴 Check in, then nap

Two hours max, set an alarm. This is the secret to beating jet lag by tonight.

16:00

⛴️ Canal cruise with Lovers, one hour, sitting down 📍

Book online at lovers.nl, the oldest canal cruise company in the city, main pier directly across from Centraal Station 🧭 Route. Around EUR 20 to 25, boats every 30 minutes. This is the perfect arrival-day move: the whole canal ring, the Golden Bend, the narrow houses and seven bridges in a row, all from a seat. Bring your own earphones, the audio guide runs through your phone on the boat WiFi and they charge EUR 1.50 for a pair.

17:15

🛍️ Nine Streets golden hour 📍 · it is late-shopping Thursday

Step off the boat straight into it. Canal walk through Singel📍 → Herengracht📍 → De 9 Straatjes, and here is the lucky timing: Thursday is Amsterdam's koopavond, the one night boutiques stay open to ~21:00. Souvenir shopping and bridges in one golden loop.

19:30

🍽️ First dinner, early night

Tomorrow has timed tickets.

💡 Buy Swiss Half Fare Cards before your first Swiss train

CHF 150 each (2026 price), valid 1 month, sold online at sbb.ch. It halves nearly every train, bus, boat and mountain railway for the next 10 days: 50% off the full Jungfraujoch round trip, the Matterhorn gondola and Pilatus. Audited against the Swiss Travel Pass: the Half Fare Card wins by roughly CHF 250 per person on this itinerary.

Good To Know · Money & Practical

The small stuff that makes the trip smooth

💶 Currencies

NL, BE and DE use the euro. Switzerland uses Swiss francs. Cards and phone-tap work almost everywhere: ~EUR 150 + CHF 150 in cash between you is plenty. At ATMs always choose "charge in local currency".

🏨 Pay-at-property

Antwerp city tax EUR 42.96 pp · Interlaken resort fee ~CHF 31.50 · Zermatt city tax CHF 36. Small amounts, zero surprises at reception.

🧥 September weather

Lowlands 12–20°C with drizzle chances, bring the compact umbrella. Swiss valleys 8–18°C. Summits at or below freezing: fleece, windproof jacket, beanie, gloves. Comfortable shoes are the most important item in the suitcase.

📱 Connectivity · the Solis rules

One Solis, three phones. It shares to up to 10 devices and doubles as a power bank, so it covers the whole crew across all four countries. Three habits make it work: (1) agree each morning who carries it, because whoever holds it holds everyone's internet, so if you split up in Zermatt or at the beach the others are offline; (2) charge it every night alongside the phones, 16 hours of battery disappears faster when it is also charging someone's iPhone; (3) download offline Google Maps for Amsterdam, Antwerp, Brugge, Gent, Cologne, Interlaken, Zermatt, Lucerne and Zurich before you fly, plus save this guide offline, so a flat Solis is an inconvenience rather than an emergency. Apps to install now: NS (NL), SNCB (BE), DB Navigator (DE) and SBB, the one you will live in for ten days.

🚆 Trains, simply

Only three legs need advance booking: the Eurocity Direct to Antwerp (nsinternational.com) and the two int.bahn.de legs, Antwerp → Cologne and Cologne → Interlaken. Everything else, including all Swiss trains, is turn-up-and-go with the Half Fare Card.

🚨 Essentials

Emergency number is 112 everywhere. Pharmacies show a green cross. Tap water is drinkable in all four countries, Swiss street fountains too. Shops close Sundays in Germany and Switzerland, stations and airports excepted.
